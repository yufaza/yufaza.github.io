Project case study

Procure-to-Pay

A business-facing web interface for SAP-backed procurement, connecting Purchase Request, optional Request for Quotation, Purchase Order, and Goods Receipt information.

01

Make the procurement journey easier to navigate

Procure-to-Pay was part of my work at PT Amerta Indah Otsuka. I developed a web interface over SAP-backed procurement information, covering Purchase Request (PR), optional Request for Quotation (RFQ), Purchase Order (PO), and Goods Receipt (GR).

The product idea was to make enterprise purchasing information easier for business users to work with through a modern web interface, while retaining its connection to the ERP process.

02

Give the business a view across procurement handovers

A purchasing need passes through several stages before goods are received. Business users need to understand where the requirement sits and how its request, supplier quotation stage, order, and receipt relate.

The interface had to express those business stages clearly while integrating the relevant SAP information. The value of the web layer lies in making the workflow understandable and accessible to its intended users.

03

Project focus

  • A web interface around the procurement flow

    The application brings PR, optional RFQ, PO, and GR information into a business-facing experience. The procurement flow can continue from PR to PO without an in-system RFQ.

  • Request for Quotation (RFQ)

    RFQ is an optional sourcing step before a purchase order. Purchasing can request quotations in the application, handle the process outside the system, or proceed without a new RFQ when an existing vendor contract applies.

  • SAP-backed integration

    Relevant procurement data remains connected to SAP rather than being presented as an unrelated standalone process.

  • Workflow-oriented presentation

    The experience follows the purchasing journey, helping users relate the current stage to the wider procurement requirement.

04

Follow the requirement through its purchasing stages

The diagram follows the business relationship between the stages. My contribution focused on the web interface and the integration connecting that experience with SAP.

05

My contribution at PT Amerta Indah Otsuka

I developed the web-based interface and worked on the application architecture integrating SAP information with the user experience. This meant translating procurement requirements into a practical application flow and connecting the business-facing screens with the relevant enterprise data.

The work formed part of my wider enterprise application development across finance, procurement, HR, and operations.

06

A business-facing layer over enterprise information

The architecture can be understood as three responsibilities: the browser presents the workflow, application integration connects the request and response, and SAP supplies the relevant enterprise records.

This case study describes the business and integration responsibilities from my contribution. It does not reproduce the former company’s internal implementation or deployment details.

07

Outcomes & lessons

The project demonstrates how a web application can make a complex ERP-backed workflow easier to approach without losing the business meaning of its stages.

The central lesson is to design the interface around the user’s purchasing question: what was requested, where supplier quotations fit, what was ordered, and what was received. Integration is useful when those relationships remain understandable.
